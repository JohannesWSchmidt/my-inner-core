2. The app

Your content stays on your iPhone

Your pillars, goals, steps and sparks, everything in your inbox, your weekly check-ins and ratings, your weekly focus and your settings are stored only on your iPhone. The widget, the Control (lock screen and Control Center) and the share extension use the same storage on your device. When you share text or a link to My Inner Core, it also lands only in your inbox on your iPhone.

There’s no user account and no server the app sends your content to. I never receive your content, and I can’t see it or recover it for you. If you delete the app, iOS deletes its data too. You can also remove all your content at once in Settings with “Delete all data”; the copies in your iCloud Drive (see below) are kept.

The app has no ads, no tracking and no third-party SDKs.

App lock with Face ID, Touch ID or passcode

If you turn on “Lock app” under Settings › Privacy, the app asks for Face ID, Touch ID or your device passcode when it opens. iOS handles the check entirely. The app only learns whether unlocking succeeded; it never gets biometric data or your passcode.

Reminders

The weekly reminder for your check-in is a local notification that iOS schedules and shows on your iPhone. Nothing is sent to me or to a server for this. You can turn it off at any time in the app or in iOS Settings.

Export and import

“Export data” creates a file (JSON) containing all of your content. You decide where to save it and who to share it with. If you email it or put it in a cloud service, for example, that provider’s terms apply. “Import data” reads such a file and replaces the data on your iPhone with it.

Automatic copies in your iCloud Drive

After a weekly check-in, the app saves a copy of your data to your own iCloud Drive, in the “My Inner Core” folder you’ll find in the Files app. This happens at most once a day. The five newest copies are kept, and the app deletes older ones.

  • What’s in them: the same as an export, so all your content, including the ratings from your weekly check-ins.
  • Who can access them: the copies are stored in your iCloud storage with Apple. Only you can access them with your Apple Account. I can’t.
  • When they’re made: only if you’re signed in to iCloud on your iPhone and iCloud Drive is turned on.
  • How to delete them: open the Files app, go to iCloud Drive › My Inner Core and delete the files there.

iCloud is an Apple service, so Apple’s terms and privacy policy apply to anything stored there (see section 4).

3. Usage statistics (only if you agree)

To see which parts of the app people use and where they get stuck, the app can send simple usage events to the analytics service Aptabase. It only does so if you agree: the app asks you once when you first set it up, and until then the statistics are off. These events never include your content. They only contain counts, yes/no values and values from fixed lists.

What’s sent

  • App opened, and from where: the app icon, widget, Control, a notification or sharing
  • Steps of the introduction on first launch, and how it ended
  • Demo started or ended
  • Weekly check-in started, cancelled or completed, with counts such as items finished, the weekly focus picked and days since the last check-in
  • Goal, step or spark created; goal achieved
  • Thought captured in or processed from the inbox
  • Weekly focus edited
  • Pillars changed (which action, and how many pillars)
  • Setting changed: reminder, app lock or color scheme
  • Help topic opened
  • Data exported or imported

Each event also includes the app’s version and build number, the iOS version, your device’s language, whether it’s a developer build, and a random session ID. The session ID is kept in memory only, never saved on the device, and replaced after an hour without events. It lets events from one session be looked at together, but can’t be used to follow a person over time.

What’s never sent

No text, no pillar names, no links, no notes and no ratings from your weekly check-ins. Also no device model, no device or advertising identifier, no cookies, no name, no email address and nothing about your Apple Account.

Who processes the data

Aptabase is operated by Technov Solutions SRL, Str. Fabricii Nr. 47, Bucharest, Romania. Aptabase processes the data on my behalf as a processor under Art. 28 GDPR (data processing agreement) and stores it in its European Union region. See Aptabase’s privacy policy for details.

When an event arrives, Aptabase necessarily sees your device’s IP address. It uses the address in memory only to work out the country (and possibly the region), and stores neither the IP address nor your system’s identifier string (user agent). According to Aptabase, it also derives a pseudonymous daily identifier from them, whose key is deleted within 48 hours.

How long it’s kept: Aptabase keeps events for up to five years. I only see aggregated reports.

Legal basis

The legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, and § 25(1) TDDDG for reading the iOS version and language on your device). You give it when you first set up the app, or later in Settings. The app works exactly the same without it.

Giving or withdrawing consent

In the app, go to Settings › Privacy › “Share usage statistics”. You can withdraw your consent there at any time, with effect for the future. While the switch is off, the app sends no events. It also sends nothing in demo mode.

4. Apple: App Store, TestFlight, iCloud

You get My Inner Core from Apple’s App Store, or as a beta through TestFlight. Apple processes data for this under its own responsibility, for example for downloads, updates and your Apple Account. All I receive from Apple is:

  • aggregated download and usage statistics that don’t identify individuals;
  • crash reports and diagnostics, if you’ve agreed in iOS Settings to share them with app developers;
  • for TestFlight: the details Apple provides for testing, such as your name and email address if I invited you by email, and any feedback and screenshots you send through TestFlight.

I use this only to fix bugs and improve the app (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

iCloud, including the automatic copies in your iCloud Drive, is Apple’s responsibility as well. See Apple’s privacy policy.

5. If you email me

If you email me, I use your email address and your message to reply (Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR). I delete your message once it’s dealt with, unless the law requires me to keep it. Please don’t send me anything from the app you’d rather keep private.

7. Your rights

Where I process your personal data, the GDPR gives you the right to

  • access that data (Art. 15),
  • have it corrected (Art. 16) or erased (Art. 17),
  • restrict its processing (Art. 18),
  • data portability (Art. 20),
  • object to processing based on legitimate interest (Art. 21), for example for hosting this website,
  • withdraw your consent, with effect for the future (Art. 7(3)), for example for the usage statistics.

Just email me at my-inner-core-app@proton.me. Keep in mind that your content from the app lives only on your iPhone and in your iCloud Drive, where you can view, export and delete it yourself. The usage statistics contain nothing I could use to link events to you, so I usually can’t tell you what’s there or delete individual entries.

Complaints: You have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77 GDPR), in particular in the EU member state where you live or work. The authority responsible for me is the Bavarian Data Protection Authority (Bayerisches Landesamt für Datenschutzaufsicht, BayLDA), Promenade 18, 91522 Ansbach, Germany, www.lda.bayern.de.
